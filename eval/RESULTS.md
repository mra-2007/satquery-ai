# SatQuery AI -- Evaluation Results

Generated by `eval/run_benchmark.py` and `eval/run_rsvqa.py`. Every number
below is the real output of those scripts on this exact codebase -- see
"How to reproduce" at the end. Full per-question detail (including the
`error` string recorded for every unsupported row) is in the CSVs each
script writes alongside itself.

## 1. BigEarthNet.txt benchmark (`eval/run_benchmark.py`)

**Sample size and scope.** `data/bench_patches/questions.csv` holds 969
questions -- a 6% sample of the full 15,029-question BigEarthNet.txt
benchmark (`data/benchmark/bigearthnet_txt_benchmark.csv`), limited to the
64 patches available locally as `data/bench_patches/*.npz` (the full reBEN
corpus is not stored locally -- see `data/README.md` / `CLAUDE.md`). Of
those 969, **654 fall into the four categories our evidence/ops.py tools
can actually answer** (presence, count, area, adjacency); the remaining
315 (reference, relative position, point, season, climate zone, country)
ask for grounding, captioning, or metadata lookup that no tool in
`agent/registry.py` provides, and are excluded from the tables below
entirely -- not scored as unsupported, since they were never in scope.

Each of the 654 in-scope questions is scored twice, against two different
masks, to separate two different sources of error:

- **PREDICTED mask** -- the real model's output (`sensor_id="fused"`,
  since these are genuine 16-channel Sentinel-1+2 stacks with real SAR).
  This is what the deployed system actually answers with.
- **GROUND-TRUTH mask** -- the patch's own annotated mask. This holds the
  reasoning layer (`classify_task` -> planner -> `validate` -> executor)
  fixed against perfect segmentation, isolating how much of the two
  tables' gap is segmentation error vs. reasoning-layer error.

### Scored against the PREDICTED mask (real model output)

| Category | N | Correct | Unsupported | Accuracy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| presence | 117 | 85 | 4 | 72.6% |
| count | 109 | 46 | 36 | 42.2% |
| area | 113 | 88 | 3 | 77.9% |
| adjacency | 315 | 180 | 27 | 57.1% |
| **Overall** | 654 | 399 | 70 | 61.0% |

### Scored against the GROUND-TRUTH mask (reasoning-layer ceiling)

| Category | N | Correct | Unsupported | Accuracy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| presence | 117 | 117 | 0 | 100.0% |
| count | 109 | 92 | 15 | 84.4% |
| area | 113 | 113 | 0 | 100.0% |
| adjacency | 315 | 313 | 0 | 99.4% |
| **Overall** | 654 | 635 | 15 | 97.1% |

### Reading the gap between the two tables

The reasoning layer alone (question parsing, `classify_task`, the
planner, `validate`, the executor) scores **97.1%** against perfect
segmentation -- essentially correct. The **36-point drop to 61.0%** on the
predicted mask is real segmentation error from the model itself, not a
flaw in the pipeline or in how these questions were parsed. `count` shows
the largest drop (84.4% -> 42.2%): component-count questions are the most
sensitive to segmentation noise, since a handful of misclassified pixels
can fragment one true region into several, or merge two into one.

### Two known, disclosed sources of the residual (non-segmentation) error

- **4-connectivity vs. 8-connectivity.** `evidence/ops.py`'s `count()` and
  `size()` use `scipy.ndimage.label`'s default 4-connectivity (only
  orthogonal neighbours join a region; diagonal touches do not) to define
  a "continuous region." BigEarthNet.txt's own annotation may use
  8-connectivity instead. This is the most likely explanation for all 4
  of the ground-truth table's wrong (non-unsupported) answers -- all 4 are
  "is this class in at most one/more than one continuous region"-style
  count/adjacency questions, e.g. "Is there no more than one continuous
  region of natural grasslands or sparsely vegetated areas visible here?"
  (ground truth "no", computed "yes"). This is a genuine methodology
  difference between two reasonable region-connectivity conventions, not
  a bug -- it affects 4 of 654 questions (0.6%) even under perfect
  segmentation.
- **Guardrail-degraded count rows.** Every `count`-category question whose
  class resolves to "Urban fabric" or "Industrial or commercial units" is
  scored unsupported (15 of 109, both tables), because
  `agent/guardrail.py`'s capability guardrail always degrades those counts
  to a `size()` call at 10 m GSD -- per CLAUDE.md, buildings (~10 m
  typical size) are not resolvable against the 25 m minimum, so no raw
  component count is ever available to compare against a count threshold.
  This is the same capability limit the RSVQA-LR eval below hits, applied
  consistently. The predicted-mask table's additional 21 count
  "unsupported" rows (36 total minus these 15) are a different thing: the
  model's own computed count/size value simply didn't fall inside any of
  a question's valid answer range/options.

## 2. RSVQA-LR (`eval/run_rsvqa.py`)

First 200 rows of `data/eval/rsvqa_lr/rsvqa_lr_test.parquet` (200 questions
over 11 distinct images), scored with the full 19-class vocabulary
(`agent/vocabulary.py`) -- not a 4-bucket collapse.

| Question type | N | Correct | Unsupported | Accuracy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| presence | 48 | 13 | 4 | 27.1% |
| count | 74 | 7 | 45 | 9.5% |
| comparison | 75 | 30 | 0 | 40.0% |
| rural_urban | 3 | 3 | 0 | 100.0% |
| **Overall** | 200 | 53 | 49 | 26.5% |

### Domain caveat: this is a fundamentally weaker input than the benchmark above

Unlike `data/bench_patches/*.npz` (real 16-channel Sentinel-1+2 stacks,
scored above), RSVQA-LR ships only an already-rendered 8-bit RGB PNG per
image. **13 of the model's 16 expected input channels are zero-filled**,
not real data:

- Only 3 channels carry real signal (the PNG's R/G/B, placed into the
  model's actual B04/B03/B02 band positions -- not a naive 1:1 copy, which
  would swap Red and Blue).
- The 8-bit pixel *values* are not real reflectance either: there is no
  way to recover Sentinel-2 digital numbers from an already-stretched,
  gamma-corrected visualization PNG, so pixel values are rescaled by a
  disclosed, uncalibrated approximation (`3000/255`) rather than left
  as-is or invented more precisely.
- `sensor_id="optical"` (not `"fused"`) -- RSVQA-LR carries no SAR signal
  at all, unlike the BigEarthNet.txt benchmark's real dual-pol Sentinel-1.

Given that, these numbers should be read as a lower bound stress-test on
badly-degraded input, not a representative measurement of the model's
real capability -- the BigEarthNet.txt tables above, run on genuine
16-channel stacks, are the fairer measurement of the model itself. A large
share of RSVQA-LR's remaining error is also vocabulary/domain mismatch
rather than segmentation error: many of its object categories (roads,
grass, residential areas, meadows, parks) have no exact match in this
model's 19-class BigEarthNet vocabulary and are resolved to the closest
real class via `agent/vocabulary.py`'s disclosed approximations (e.g.
"roads" -> "Urban fabric") rather than refused outright, but that mapping
is itself imprecise by construction. `count`'s 45 unsupported rows are
predominantly the same guardrail-degrade limit described above (Urban
fabric/Industrial units uncountable at 10 m GSD).
